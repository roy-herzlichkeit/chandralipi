# Real cross-mission matches, 2026-09-08/09

**Not synthetic. Not ISRO.** ISRO/PRADAN access was blocked by poor network
conditions the night before a POC showcase, so these pairs substitute other
real, public, no-login datasets — **JAXA's Kaguya (SELENE) Terrain Camera**
and **NASA's LRO WAC** — matched against each other, or against a second
independent Kaguya pass. Nothing here is generated imagery, and nothing here
is labelled as Chandrayaan-2 data. Every sensor name in the results store and
on the dashboard is the real one: `JAXA_SELENE_TC`, `LRO_WAC`.

Both instruments were reachable in well under 250 MB of total transfer for
everything below — see "Why NASA/JAXA and not ISRO" at the end.

## The two pairs

### 1. JAXA vs JAXA — same sensor, two independent passes

Two real Kaguya Terrain Camera scenes, **~2 hours apart in acquisition time**,
over genuinely overlapping ground (confirmed from their PDS footprints, not
assumed): `TC1S2B0_01_05504N194E0230` and `TC1S2B0_01_05505N193E0219`, lon
~21–24°E, lat ~18–20°N, ~10.8 m/px native. Both already share the same CRS
(Equirectangular, clon=0), so no reprojection was needed — just a percentile
contrast stretch to uint8 and a downsize to fit within LoFTR's measured
1152 px CPU tile budget.

**Real results:**

| matcher | matches | inliers | inlier ratio | RMSE (px) | median (px) | uniformity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SIFT** | 542 | 542 | 100% | **0.46** | **0.33 — sub-pixel** | 0.51 |
| AKAZE | 279 | 279 | 100% | 0.71 | 0.42 | 0.46 |
| ASIFT | 3660 | 3659 | 99.97% | 0.70 | 0.44 | 0.50 |
| LightGlue | 275 | 274 | 99.6% | 0.80 | 0.63 | 0.49 |
| LoFTR | 1783 | 1762 | 98.8% | 6.05 | 5.94 | 0.49 |

SIFT's 0.33 px median is the strongest sub-pixel result anywhere in this
project's real-data work — expected, since removing the cross-instrument
scale gap removes the hardest part of the problem. None of the five pass the
0.7 uniformity gate (coverage sits around 0.35–0.38 across the board): the
correspondences cluster where the shared crater/rille structure is, not
evenly across the whole frame. A real, honestly-reported limitation, not
hidden by the strong residual numbers above it.

### 2. JAXA vs NASA WAC — the real cross-instrument pair

`TC1S2B0_01_05600N005E1008` (~5.9 m/px) vs the LRO WAC global mosaic
(100 m/px), lon ~99.3–101.4°E, lat ~-0.5–1.3°N. TC reprojected from its native
Equirectangular (clon=180) CRS onto WAC's CRS (clon=0) via
`preprocess.georeference()`, then resampled to WAC's 100 m/px via
`preprocess.resample.to_common_gsd()` — both real project functions, not
one-off code. Matched with `pipeline.register_pair()` (RANSAC + ECC refine +
full metrics), the actual project pipeline.

**Real results:**

| matcher | matches | inliers | inlier ratio | RMSE (px) | median (px) | uniformity | gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LightGlue** | 741 | 719 | 97.0% | 2.31 | 1.55 | **0.85** | **PASS** |
| ASIFT | 29 | 29 | 100% | 2.21 | 0.77 | 0.24 | fail |
| SIFT | 16 | 16 | 100% | 3.05 | 0.82 | 0.25 | fail |
| LoFTR | 17 | 13 | 76.5% | 4.03 | 3.56 | 0.29 | fail |
| AKAZE | 1 | — | — | — | — | **refused (`TOO_FEW_MATCHES`)**, not padded |

LightGlue is the headline: 719 well-spread inliers, the only matcher whose
correspondences pass the uniformity gate. Visually, the match lines carpet
the shared crater structure and the checkerboard shows crater rims and
shading continuing cleanly across every tile boundary. LightGlue initially
failed outright here — DISK's U-Net backbone requires image dimensions
divisible by 16, and neither crop was — fixed by zero-padding both images at
the bottom/right only, so existing pixel coordinates stay valid.

## A negative result, kept rather than discarded

A second location was deliberately hunted for — `TC1S2B0_01_05660N099E0382`,
chosen after previewing dozens of candidates for a large, distinctive crater
plus a sinuous rille, both clearly visible in a decimated preview at both
native TC resolution and after resampling to WAC's 100 m/px. Visually it
looked stronger than pair 2 above.

**It matched badly.** LightGlue and LoFTR both reported deceptively
high-confidence numbers (932 and 1482 matches, 100% and 99.6% "inlier
ratio") — but RMSE of 53 px and 72 px. The checkerboard showed the crater
rims genuinely misaligned across every tile boundary; this was **caught by
looking at the actual image, not by trusting the metrics table**, which is
exactly the discipline this project's evidence-labelling rule exists to
enforce. The cause: a large, rotationally-symmetric crater is a poor
matching target — every point on its rim looks like every other point on
its rim, and a second similar-sized crater nearby in the WAC frame gave the
matchers a second, wrong place to lock onto. The two overwritten `.npz`
files (`JAXA_SELENE_TC-LRO_WAC_{lightglue,loftr}`) were restored byte-for-byte
from the surviving source arrays once this was caught, and the restored
numbers were verified to match the original run exactly. Nothing from this
attempt is in the results store; it is recorded here only as a finding worth
repeating if asked "did you try other locations."

## Two bugs found and fixed in the project's own tooling while building this

1. **`scripts/export_web_data.py` could silently emit invalid JSON.** A
   string-typed field (`ecc_prefilter`) could come back as pandas `NaN` when
   one result's `extra` dict lacked a key another result had set — Python's
   `json.dump` happily writes a bare `NaN` token, which Python's own
   `json.load` accepts silently but which is not valid per the JSON spec and
   breaks every browser's `JSON.parse`. Fixed with a proper NaN-guard on
   string fields, and `allow_nan=False` on the final `json.dumps` so any
   future occurrence fails loudly at export time instead of shipping a page
   that says "No exported results found."
2. **`scripts/build_demo_results.py` → `save_results()` rebuilds the index
   from only its own batch, unmerged.** Running it after real results exist
   silently drops them from `index.parquet` (their `.npz` files survive on
   disk, just unindexed) — this is exactly what happened once mid-session.
   **Not fixed** — flagged only, since the user asked to find the cause
   before touching shared pipeline code under deadline pressure.

## Why NASA/JAXA and not ISRO, concretely

- **PRADAN** requires login and its cart has no location filter — the carts
  built this week returned 500 unfiltered files per instrument spanning the
  entire mission; even one OHRC product is ~750 MB.
- **JAXA (Kaguya TC)** is a public AWS S3 bucket, `--no-sign-request`. A 39 MB
  STAC-style parquet index was queried locally to find genuinely overlapping
  tiles *before* downloading anything; only the specific ~20–65 MB tiles that
  mattered were ever pulled.
- **NASA (LRO WAC)**: the reference mosaic is 5.96 GB, but it's a plain HTTPS
  file with `Accept-Ranges: bytes`. It was never downloaded whole — GDAL
  fetched only the ~50–100 MB full-width row-band covering each target
  location, cropped locally to the needed columns.

Total real data pulled across every attempt in this section: well under
1 GB, versus PRADAN's multi-hundred-MB-per-file, login-gated, unfiltered
carts. That is the literal bandwidth reason this worked on poor wifi and
PRADAN did not.

## Where this lives

Saved into the normal results store (`data/processed/results/`,
`synthetic=False` on every entry) — **9 rows total**, none of them
Chandrayaan-2. Exported to `web/public/data/results.json`. Streamlit reads
the `.npz` store directly; clicking a row on either dashboard shows the
actual matched source/reference images plus the correspondence,
checkerboard, and trust-map views.
